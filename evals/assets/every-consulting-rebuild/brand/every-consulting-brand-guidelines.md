# Every Consulting — Brand Visual Guidelines (Consulting / Teal)

> The visual identity for **Every Consulting** client-facing decks. This is the brand
> reference you rebuild the deck *into*: the exact colors, typography, layout language,
> texture, art treatment, and the things the brand refuses. It is tool-agnostic — it
> describes the *look to achieve*, not how to build it. Achieve it with whatever your
> toolchain provides.
>
> Bundled assets (in this folder): `stipple-texture-overlay.png` (the teal grain),
> `every-logo-white.png` (footer logo), and `art/` (classical black-and-white
> illustrations). Use them — don't fabricate substitutes.

## Overall style identity — "Modern Classical Editorial"

Every's visual language blends:
- **Classical/Renaissance imagery** — Greek architecture, Roman figures, stippled engravings
- **Modern dark-mode sensibility** — deep backgrounds, clean typography
- **Editorial magazine quality** — serif type, careful spacing, a literary feel
- **Organic artistic touches** — smooth curved sweeps, hand-drawn quality

The tension that makes it distinctive: *ancient imagery + modern presentation; dark, serious
backgrounds + selective playful accents; editorial formality + organic informality.*

This is the **Consulting / Teal** theme — the client-facing variant. Mood: sophisticated,
professional, *expert but welcoming* — appropriate for client training.

## Color palette (exact hexes)

**Core:**

| Name | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Teal Dark | `#0F5258` | Primary background, dark accents |
| Teal Medium | `#4999A0` | Secondary backgrounds, depth, card gradients |
| Warm White | `#FFFEFB` | Primary text, cards (softer than pure white) |
| Pure White | `#FFFFFF` | Text highlights |
| Muted Light Teal | `#C3DCDE` | **Secondary text (captions, labels, eyebrows, footers) — the READABILITY FLOOR.** Nothing dimmer than this over the texture. Use ONE shared value for all secondary text. |

**Accents** (used intentionally, never gratuitously — mainly as category pill borders / highlights):

| Hex | Color | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| `#BB7B19` | Gold/Amber | Accent, pill borders, emphasis |
| `#F8DE6E` | Light Gold | Highlights |
| `#2E8D23` | Green | Accent, pill borders |

Color philosophy: dark-mode-first, selective accent use, organic integration (colors live in
curves and pills, not hard-edged blocks), texture over flat.

## Typography

- **Primary (serif):** Signifier — high stroke contrast, editorial. Not web-safe; for slide
  generation use **Georgia** (closest web-safe editorial serif), fallback Times New Roman.
  Serif carries headlines AND body.
- **Secondary (sans):** Switzer / DM Sans for small UI elements — labels, session indicators,
  footers. (Arial is an acceptable web-safe stand-in.)
- **Case:** Sentence case for all headlines and body. ALL CAPS only for the EVERY logo and
  short eyebrow/section labels. Title Case occasionally in agendas.
- **Weight:** regular serif headlines; **bold single words** for emphasis ("we **live** in the
  future"); italic for taglines and "Consulting" in the logo.

## Layout language

- **50/50 (or 40/60) splits** — content on one side, classical illustration on the other.
- **Generous margins and whitespace** — content never crowds the edges; one focal point per slide.
- **Soft containment** — rounded-rectangle cards (often frosted/semi-transparent with a teal
  gradient), pill-shaped badges, organic blob text containers. No sharp rectangular frames.
- **Common slide types:** title/section (eyebrow label, large serif headline, atmospheric art);
  agenda/list (title left, cards/pills right); content+illustration split; quote/emphasis
  (text in an organic shape, art opposite, maximum breathing room).

**Footer (consistent on every slide):** `EVERY` + italic *Consulting* on the **left**
(use `every-logo-white.png`), session/section indicator on the **right**, secondary-teal color.

## Background & texture (this is essential, not optional)

A flat teal fill reads as corporate and is wrong. Every Consulting backgrounds are deep teal
`#0F5258` carrying the **stipple texture's macro contour bands** — the dune-like sweeps that
are the brand vibe.

Rules:
1. **Never zoom into the texture.** The contour bands ARE the brand. Scaling up crops inside a
   band and yields characterless dot-grain. Per-slide variety comes from **vertical pan +
   flip/mirror** of a full-width window of `stipple-texture-overlay.png`, never from zoom.
2. **Keep it quiet under text** — composite at roughly **10% strength**. Louder puts grain
   under glyphs and hurts readability.

The intended result: each slide's background is teal with a soft full-width contour sweep, the
texture varied slide-to-slide by panning/mirroring, never loud enough to fight the text.

## Art treatment

- **Black-and-white classical illustrations only** (Theme 2) — Greek/Roman figures,
  architecture, busts, colosseum/temples, often paired with a modern element (laptop, robot).
  Transparent backgrounds so the teal shows through. Stippled/pointillist rendering.
- Use the bundled `art/` set; place as atmospheric 50/50 companions, not decoration crammed
  into corners. Preserve each image's aspect ratio.
- **Organic swoosh (Theme 2):** smooth curved shapes in a darker teal for depth layers — refined,
  not the painterly brushstroke of the internal theme.

## Categorized lists / pills

Pill-shaped badges with **colored borders** (gold, green) signal categories — dark fill inside,
centered text, the border color carrying the category. This is the Consulting theme's signature
list treatment in place of bullets.

## What the brand REFUSES (hard avoid-list)

- Corporate blue / gray; stock photography; neon gradients; geometric pattern fills
- Triangles, hexagons, diagonal slashes, hard angular dividers, corner brackets, rigid grids
- Sans-serif for main/body content; ALL-CAPS headlines; rotated/outlined/stroke text
- Heavy or double-line borders, drop shadows on boxes, decorative borders
- Gradient fills as primary backgrounds (gradients live only inside illustrations/cards)
- Busy, cluttered layouts; content crowding the edges

> If a slide could belong to a generic corporate or tech-startup template, it isn't on-brand yet.
> The test: it should look like it could only be an Every Consulting slide — editorial serif on
> textured teal, classical art, selective accent pills, generous air.
